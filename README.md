# Pajares Mountain Corridor Baseline

Climate and vegetation conditions at Puerto de Pajares (1370 m), a mountain pass in
the Cantabrian range crossed by a transport and energy corridor — the AP-66
motorway, a railway, and high-voltage transmission lines.

Built entirely on open data, with every assumption recorded.

---

## The question

**How have maximum temperature and vegetation cover changed at this pass, and can
that be measured reproducibly from open data?**

Standard remote-sensing workflows are developed on flat terrain and degrade in
relief in ways that are predictable but rarely handled. At 43° N on a watershed at
1370 m, the sun drops to 21° in December, north-facing slopes fall into their own
shadow, snow is classified as cloud, and adjacent climate grid cells differ by more
than the entire century-scale warming signal.

Measuring that degradation is part of the result.

---

## Results

### Maximum temperature is rising

TerraClimate monthly maximum 2-m temperature, 1970–2025, 672 monthly values:

| | value |
|---|---|
| Trend | **+0.48 °C per decade** |
| Total change 1970–2025 | **+2.66 °C** |
| Mean of monthly maxima | 12.32 °C |

Above the Spanish average of 0.3–0.4 °C per decade, consistent with faster warming
at altitude.

### The trend is robust; the absolute value is not

Two adjacent grid cells, 4.6 km apart, straddling the watershed:

| | South cell | North cell | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean | 12.32 °C | 15.60 °C | **3.28 °C** |
| Trend | +0.48 °C/decade | +0.45 °C/decade | **0.03 °C/decade** |

Verified rather than assumed: **absolute temperatures from a 4 km climate grid
cannot be attached to a point in mountainous terrain; changes over time can.**

### Vegetation shows a closed, seasonally active canopy

Sentinel-2 NDVI, 2023, after quality control (48 acquisitions → 28 usable):

- February minimum 0.49 — deciduous canopy leaf-off over evergreen understorey
- May rise 0.59 → 0.87 in one month — leaf-out
- June–October plateau 0.85–0.90 — closed canopy, NDVI saturated

Saturation is itself a finding: NDVI establishes that closed forest is present but
cannot measure its height.

### Regional context

Landsat 8 annual median composites over Asturias, 2020 and 2025, Tier 1 only,
identical processing and display scale. Distributions matched to within 0.002 at
every percentile — a uniform shift, which is not how a change in surface cover
presents. Recorded as a negative result: RGB composites of two years cannot
separate a radiometric shift from real change.

## Method

| Step | Notebook |
|---|---|
| Administrative boundaries, 78 concejos | `notebooks/01_boundaries.ipynb` |
| Climate trend, 1970–2025 | `notebooks/02_climate_trend.ipynb` |
| NDVI annual cycle with quality control | `notebooks/03_vegetation_cycle.ipynb` |
| Regional composites, 2020 vs 2025 | `notebooks/04_regional_composites.ipynb` |

Processing order, with the two additions that tutorial material omits:
```text
1. Search STAC, inventory metadata, decide parameters on the counts
2. Load, mask NoData, convert to reflectance
3. Quality mask - clouds AND terrain shadow AND snow          [addition]
4. Compute the index
5. Extract the point or the area
6. Reject implausible values                                  [addition]
7. Resample to a regular grid
8. Interpolate inside the series only - never fill the ends    [changed]
9. Smooth
10. Leave gaps where no data exists                            [changed]
```
A smoothing filter does not remove an error. It spreads the error across the
neighbours and makes it invisible. Quality control therefore precedes smoothing.

Full reasoning in [`docs/METHODOLOGY.md`](docs/METHODOLOGY.md).

## Limitations

Stated explicitly, because the argument is stronger for containing them.

- **One year, one point, one site.** The NDVI series covers 2023 at a single
  location. Multi-year series and a second site are not done.
- **NDVI saturates** above roughly 0.85 and cannot measure canopy height.
- **TerraClimate is a modelled product**, not a measurement. Its complete absence
  of gaps reflects the method, not the reliability of any value.
- **Winter is empty.** Cloud, terrain shadow and low sun remove observations in the
  same months. Reported rather than filled.
- **Cloud masking is biased in this terrain.** The scene classification layer
  over-flags steep slopes at low sun, so data loss concentrates where the corridor
  runs.

Full list with measured values in [`docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md`](docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md).

## Reproducing

Notebooks run in Google Colab without setup — each installs its own dependencies.

To run locally:

```bash
conda env create -f environment.yml
conda activate python_remote_sensing
jupyter lab
```

Run the notebooks in numerical order. Set the output folder at the top of each.

## Context

This repository establishes conditions. A separate project assesses what those
conditions mean for the transmission infrastructure crossing the pass — which
requires conductor specifications, span geometry and a thermal model, none of which
are present here.

The two share a subject: transmission corridors in mountainous terrain, where
relief and climate depart far enough from the conditions standard methods assume
that the methods themselves must be adapted.

Reasoning in [`docs/climate-rationale.md`](docs/climate-rationale.md).
